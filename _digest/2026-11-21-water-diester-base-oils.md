## What's new

The authors asked how a very small amount of water sits inside synthetic ester lubricants before it forms clusters. They simulated one water molecule in each of two diester base oils, bis(2-ethylhexyl) adipate (DEHA) and bis(2-ethylhexyl) sebacate (DEHS), at 343 K. Each oil had three independent 12 ns molecular dynamics (MD) runs.

Carbonyl oxygen atoms accounted for about 98% of the water–ester hydrogen bonds. In 13.66% (DEHA) and 12.02% (DEHS) of frames, the two water hydrogens bonded to two different ester molecules at once. Individual hydrogen-bond events lasted about 0.19 ps in both oils. DEHA showed higher mean hydrogen-bond and carbonyl-neighbor counts, while DEHS had a higher density-normalized coordination integral.

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations on 12 targeted water–ester dimers found all of them attractive. Changing the basis set from 6-31+G(d,p) to def2-TZVPD shifted interaction energies by 0.306–1.607 kJ/mol.

## Why it matters

Moisture changes how ester lubricants age and behave, but the first step, where one water molecule goes, is rarely described in molecular terms. The paper separates three descriptors: whether contact occurs, how molecules are connected, and how long contacts persist. For researchers running MD or reactive simulations of lubricants, this gives a reference point and a worked example of cross-checking classical trajectories with quantum calculations.

## Caveats

The study covers a single water molecule per simulation, two oils, and one temperature. The authors state that it does not establish ensemble binding-strength rankings or macroscopic performance differences between the oils. Only the abstract was read for this summary, so details of the force field and the DFT setup are not covered here.
